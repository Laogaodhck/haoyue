Controls the user's computer: screen capture, mouse, keyboard, clipboard, windows, and app launch. Coordinates are pixels in the most recent screenshot; the runtime maps them to the real display (DPI and scaling included), even when the image was downsampled.

Actions: 'screenshot' (capture and attach the screen); 'left_click' / 'right_click' / 'middle_click' / 'double_click' / 'triple_click' with 'coordinate' [x, y] (triple_click selects a paragraph or line); 'element_click' with 'element_id' (click a control found by computer_inspect — more reliable than pixel coordinates); 'drag' with 'coordinate' [x1, y1] and 'destination' [x2, y2]; 'mouse_move'; 'cursor_position' (reports the cursor in screen pixels and in last-screenshot pixels, useful to calibrate); 'type' with 'text' (\n produces Enter, \t produces Tab); 'key' with 'text' as a combo like 'ctrl+s', 'enter', 'alt+tab', 'win', 'f5'; 'scroll' with 'delta_y' in wheel notches (positive = scroll down, negative = up; 'delta_x' for horizontal) and optional 'coordinate' anchor; 'wait' with 'duration_ms' (max 15000) after opening apps or menus; 'clipboard_set' / 'clipboard_get' with 'text' for set — prefer clipboard_set followed by key 'ctrl+v' over 'type' for long text, passwords fields, or CJK-heavy content; 'open' with 'text' naming an app ('calc'), file path, or URL; 'focus_window' and 'list_windows'.

Prefer 'element_click' when computer_inspect listed the target; fall back to screenshot coordinates otherwise. After any state-changing action, either pass 'auto_screenshot': true or take a 'screenshot' before deciding the next step. One action per call.
